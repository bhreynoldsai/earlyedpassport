# The Compass — starter activity library

12 original activities, two per Pathway, written from scratch for Early Ed
Passport. See `docs/FRAMEWORK.md` for the framework these tag into, and
`lib/framework/seed-data.ts` for the Skill Markers themselves. This is a
starter set — `docs/OPEN-ITEMS.md` already budgets a much larger activity
library as ongoing content work, same as it did before this framework
existed.

Every activity carries the same five fields on purpose — Title, Age
Band(s) + Skill Marker(s), Materials, Try This, Take It Home, and a
Grown-up Tip — which is intentionally richer than DECAL's own activity
library (`docs/reference/gelds-activity-library.md`), where materials are
buried in prose and there's no family-facing field at all.

## Curious Mind

### What's Under the Scarf?

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 12–24 months — CM-3.1, CM-1.2

**Materials:** 1 soft scarf or lightweight cloth · 2–3 of the child's
favorite small toys · a small basket to hold the toys

**Try This:** Sit facing the child with a toy in view. Let them watch you
slowly cover it with the scarf, then ask, "Where did it go?" Wait a few
seconds to see if they reach for or pull off the scarf. If they need a
nudge, peek under it yourself and act surprised when you find it. Repeat
with a new toy each time, and once they've got the hang of it, try hiding
two toys under two scarves for a bigger challenge.

**Take It Home:** Play the same hiding game at snack time with a napkin
over a cracker or small toy — it turns a few spare minutes into a
searching game.

**Grown-up Tip:** If a child doesn't search yet, that's completely normal
— this skill often clicks somewhere in the second half of the first year.
Any glance, reach, or pull at the cloth counts as real thinking in
progress, so celebrate it, even if they don't fully "solve" it yet.

### Sink or Float Lab

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 36–60 months — CM-1.4, CM-3.4

**Materials:** A clear plastic bin filled partway with water · 6–8 safe
household objects (a cork, a spoon, a sponge, a small rock, a plastic
bottle cap, a crayon) · a towel for spills · two trays or hoops labeled
"sink" and "float"

**Try This:** Hold up one object at a time and ask, "What do you think
will happen — sink or float?" Let each child answer before you drop it in.
After it settles, sort it into the right tray together and ask, "Why do
you think that happened?" There's no need to correct wrong guesses — the
guessing and testing is the whole point. Five objects is plenty for one
sitting if time is short.

**Take It Home:** Try the same game with bath toys and a few kitchen items
during bath time — ask your child to guess before each splash.

**Grown-up Tip:** Resist the urge to explain the "right" science answer
right away. A child who tries a second guess after being wrong, or changes
their reasoning, is doing exactly the kind of thinking this activity is
meant to build.

## Growing Strong

### Stepping Stone Adventure

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 12–24 months (GS-1.2), 24–36 months
(GS-1.3)

**Materials:** 6–8 carpet squares or sturdy paper plates · painter's tape
(optional) · open floor space

**Try This:** Lay the carpet squares in a loose, curving path across the
floor, spaced for small steps. Invite children to walk (or, for the older
ones, run and stop) from square to square, cheering them on as they go.
Change the path each day — a straight line, a zigzag, a loop — to keep it
fresh.

**Take It Home:** Make a stepping-stone path from couch cushions or towels
across the living room floor for a rainy-day game.

**Grown-up Tip:** Some children will want to run straight through instead
of stepping carefully — that's great gross-motor practice too. Widen the
spacing for new walkers, narrow it for children ready for a balance
challenge.

### Bubble Hands

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 24–36 months (GS-3.3), 36–48 months
(GS-2.4)

**Materials:** Mild soap · a low sink or basin of warm water · a small
hand towel per child · a simple picture chart of the hand-washing steps

**Try This:** Bring small groups to the sink before snack time. Walk
through each step together — wet, soap, scrub (count to ten while singing
a short song), rinse, dry — pointing to the picture chart as you go. Let
children pump the soap and turn the water on and off themselves; that
push-and-turn motion builds the same hand control they'll use for tools
like scissors.

**Take It Home:** Ask families to let their child pump their own soap and
dry their own hands at home, even if it's a little slower and a little
splashy.

**Grown-up Tip:** It's normal for this to take longer than you'd expect at
first. Building the habit matters more than doing it fast.

## Finding Words

### The Feelings Walk

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 24–36 months (FW-2.3), 36–48 months
(FW-2.4)

**Materials:** None required (optional: a small basket of props — a toy
phone, a stuffed animal, a mirror)

**Try This:** Take a slow lap around the classroom or hallway with one or
two children at a time. Stop at ordinary things and ask a simple, open
question: "How does this make you feel?" or "What do you think is
happening here?" Give plenty of quiet space to answer. When a child
answers, echo it back with one more word added, like "Sunny — that window
feels sunny!"

**Take It Home:** Families can try their own "feelings walk" on the way to
the car or the mailbox, asking their child what they notice and how it
makes them feel.

**Grown-up Tip:** Don't rush to fill silence — toddlers often need a few
extra seconds to find their words. If a child answers in a home language
other than English, that counts too; celebrate it and repeat it back
warmly. Growing up hearing more than one language is a strength, not
something to correct.

### Build-a-Story Basket

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 36–48 months (FW-1.5), 48–60 months
(FW-2.5, FW-3.3)

**Materials:** A small basket or bag · 4–5 familiar objects or toy figures
(a toy dog, a spoon, a small car, a block, a play person)

**Try This:** Gather 3–4 children in a circle and pass the basket around.
Each child pulls out one object without looking. Start a story together
out loud — "Once there was a dog who found a spoon..." — and let each
child add the next part when their object comes up. When the story ends,
ask one child to try retelling the whole thing from the start.

**Take It Home:** Suggest families keep a "story basket" of small
household objects by the dinner table and take turns adding a line to a
silly made-up story.

**Grown-up Tip:** If a child gets stuck, offer two simple choices ("Does
the dog run, or does the dog nap?") instead of an open-ended prompt — it
keeps the story moving and builds their confidence for next time.

## Big Feelings, Good Friends

### Mirror, Mirror, Who's That?

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 0–12 months (BF-1.1, BF-2.1), 12–24
months (BF-1.2)

**Materials:** One unbreakable baby mirror (or a smartphone camera flipped
to selfie mode) · a soft, low-light corner or rug space · optional lovey
or blanket

**Try This:** Sit with the child in your lap facing a mirror at floor
level. Point to their reflection and say their name. Narrate what you see:
"Look at your hands. Look at that big smile." Let them lead — some
children will reach out and touch the glass, others will just stare, and
both are wins. Keep it to about 5 minutes.

**Take It Home:** Try the same game at your own bathroom mirror during
getting-dressed time.

**Grown-up Tip:** If a child gets fussy partway through, switch to holding
and soothing instead of pushing the activity — the connection matters more
than finishing the game.

### The Calm-Down Cove

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 36–48 months (BF-2.4), 48–60 months
(BF-2.5, BF-3.5)

**Materials:** A small tent, canopy, or blanket draped over a table · 2–3
soft cushions · a "feelings jar" or a laminated card with 3 breathing steps
in pictures · a basket with two hand puppets

**Try This:** Set up the Cove in a low-traffic corner ahead of time.
Introduce it during circle time as a spot anyone can visit when they need
a minute — not a time-out, a landing pad. Model it yourself first. When a
child is heading toward a meltdown, offer it as a choice: "Want to go to
the Cove, or do you want me to sit with you here?" For two children in a
tiff, the puppets can act out the disagreement first.

**Take It Home:** Set up a mini calm-down spot at home, and let your child
name it themselves so it feels like theirs.

**Grown-up Tip:** Some children need company to calm down and some need
space — watch what actually works for each child. If a child never uses it
alone, that's not a failure of the activity, that's useful information.

## Trying & Doing

### The Wonder Box

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 24–36 months (TD-1.3), 36–48 months
(TD-1.4)

**Materials:** 1 shoebox with a hand-sized hole cut in one end · 5 safe,
different-textured objects (pinecone, sponge, soft ball, fabric scrap,
wooden block) · a small towel or scarf to cover the hole between turns

**Try This:** Drop one object into the box without showing the child.
Invite them to reach in, feel it, and guess what it is before pulling it
out to check. If a group is playing, let kids ask each other one question
about what they felt before guessing.

**Take It Home:** Fill a paper bag with three items from around the house
and take turns guessing by feel.

**Grown-up Tip:** Some children will want to peek, and that's okay — the
goal is curious exploring, not a strict "no looking" rule.

### Button, Zip, Snap

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 36–48 months (TD-2.4), 48–60 months
(TD-3.4)

**Materials:** An old shirt or jacket with a working zipper, a few large
buttons, and a snap (or a fastening board) · a low table or floor mat · a
small basket

**Try This:** Set the item out during choice time. Let each child work the
zipper, buttons, and snap at their own pace. If they get stuck, offer a
small hint instead of finishing it for them. Celebrate the attempt, not
just the finished zip.

**Take It Home:** Build in an extra minute during getting-dressed time for
your child to try their own buttons or zipper before you step in.

**Grown-up Tip:** The urge to just do it for them is real, especially when
short on time. Try narrating their effort out loud instead — "You're
figuring out which way it goes" — it keeps their hands working.

## Wonder & Make

### Squish & Swirl Paint Bags

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 24–36 months (WM-1.3), 36–48 months
(WM-1.4)

**Materials:** A quart-size zip-top bag (one per child) · 2–3 dabs of
washable paint, different colors · clear packing tape · a flat table or
tray

**Try This:** Squeeze a little paint into each bag, press out the air, and
seal it well, reinforced with tape. Tape the bag flat to the table. Kids
swirl, smoosh, and drag the paint around with their fingers without ever
getting messy hands. Ask, "What do you see now?" and let them name it.

**Take It Home:** Try the same trick with a bag of hair gel and a few
sequins or buttons dropped inside.

**Grown-up Tip:** There's no "finished" painting to save here — the magic
is in the squishing, not the picture.

### Box Band Parade

**Age Band(s) & Skill Marker(s):** 12–24 months (WM-3.2), 36–48 months
(WM-3.4)

**Materials:** Empty oatmeal containers or small cereal boxes (one per
child, as drums) · wooden spoons or unsharpened pencils · a few scarves or
ribbon streamers · a speaker or phone for music

**Try This:** Hand out the "drums" and let kids bang out their own rhythm
to a favorite song. After a song or two, swap the drumsticks for scarves
and parade around the room. Every so often, pause the music and ask, "Can
you make a new sound nobody's heard yet?"

**Take It Home:** Turn pots, pans, and wooden spoons into a five-minute
kitchen dance party while dinner's on the stove.

**Grown-up Tip:** Resist the urge to help them "keep the beat." A toddler
banging off-rhythm with total joy is exactly the point.
